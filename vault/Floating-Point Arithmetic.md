Computers cannot represent the infinite continuum of real numbers. They must approximate them using a finite discrete set of values. Floating-Point arithmetic is the system used to perform math on these discrete approximations. 

Because its not exact, standard algebraic rules sometimes fail. For example, 0.1+0.2-0.3 does not equal 0 in floating point arithmetic due to small errors in how the computer represents these values

Floating-point operations are distinguished from regular exact math operations with a circle around them: $\oplus, \ominus, \odot, \oslash$
